# Silent Cradle — 3-Act Shell (v1)

Date: 2026-02-22  
Runtime target: ~90 min (flexible)
Tone target: A Separation-style realism (moral complexity, no clean villains)

## Core dramatic thesis
Lina’s fear of social shame around childlessness drives her from private coping into public moral collapse, where peer and institutional pressure harden everyone’s positions. The ending stays ambiguous about her belief in the akuaba, but clear about the relational cost of her choices.

---

## ACT 1 — SETUP + ATTACHMENT (approx. 25–30 min)

### Function
Establish Lina/Rob’s emotional fracture, social pressure ecosystem, and the akuaba as a plausible coping object that gradually becomes identity-bearing.

### Story movement
- Introduce Lina as competent teacher, quietly unraveling under childlessness grief.
- Show peer/institution pressure in subtle forms (staff culture, family comments, school optics, implied timelines).
- Lina encounters akuaba through Kwame (not magical framing; grounded, culturally contextualized).
- Object begins as comfort ritual.
- Rob initially supports Lina’s coping but avoids direct emotional processing.

### Act 1 turning points
1. **Inciting incident:** a social/institutional trigger intensifies Lina’s shame (e.g., school/family event centered on motherhood).
2. **Lock-in to ritual:** Lina adopts private akuaba routine as emotional regulator.
3. **First threshold (Act break):** Lina makes a consequential choice that prioritizes object-mediated control over relational openness.

### End of Act 1 state
- Lina feels “held” by ritual.
- Rob feels shut out but still conciliatory.
- The audience can still read Lina’s behavior as understandable coping.

---

## ACT 2 — ESCALATION + MORAL COLLISION (approx. 40–45 min)

### Function
Escalate from coping to compulsion. Build no-villain conflict where each character’s reasonable action worsens outcomes.

### Story movement
- Lina’s rituals become less private and more behavior-governing.
- School environment starts reflecting stress: classroom symbolism (red-pen motif), boundary slips, authority strain.
- Peer pressure and institutional pressure combine (concern framed as accountability / professionalism / reputation).
- Elena intervenes from care, but oversteps.
- Rob shifts from passive support to alarm and control.
- Dr. Chen offers professional framing; Lina resists being reduced to pathology.

### Midpoint (moral pivot)
- A scene where Lina receives an opening for honest repair but chooses control/image protection instead.
- Stakes shift from “will she heal?” to “what will this cost everyone?”

### Act 2 pressure ladder
- Conflicting versions of events emerge (home, school, peers).
- Trust fractures; social exposure risk rises.
- Lina’s actions, meant to avoid shame, begin creating larger public shame.

### End of Act 2 state (dark turn)
- Relationship rupture is now active, not latent.
- Institutional consequences are imminent.
- Lina is morally cornered: surrender control and face shame, or escalate and lose more.

---

## ACT 3 — RECKONING + AMBIGUOUS RESOLUTION (approx. 18–22 min)

### Function
Force a final moral choice, show irreversible relational consequences, and leave spiritual/object belief unresolved but ethically charged.

### Story movement
- Confrontation sequence where all parties act from legitimate values (care, truth, dignity, boundaries, safety).
- Lina is pushed to name what she has protected all along: ego/self-image against childlessness shame.
- She makes a bounded truth choice (not total confession spectacle, not total denial).

### Climax
- A high-pressure scene where Lina can either preserve image or preserve remaining human connection.
- She chooses connection partially, but too late to avoid full damage.

### Ending design (ambiguity with clarity)
- **Ambiguous:** audience cannot fully determine whether Lina still believes the akuaba has active power, symbolic meaning, or both.
- **Clear:** the film underlines the lesson that she sacrificed key relationships while avoiding shame.
- **Final note:** no neat closure; only altered trajectory and costly self-recognition.

---

## Character arc compression (for shell)
- **Lina:** grief -> ritual control -> public/private fracture -> partial truth -> uncertain spiritual stance.
- **Rob:** avoidant support -> reactive control -> moral fatigue -> conditional re-engagement.
- **Elena:** caring intervention -> boundary breach -> accountability.
- **Institution/peers (system):** concern -> scrutiny -> pressure that intensifies collapse.

---

## Sequence map (90-min guiding split)
- Seq 1-2 (Act 1): world, wound, object entry, first lock-in
- Seq 3-6 (Act 2): escalation across home/school/social systems, midpoint moral pivot, dark turn
- Seq 7-8 (Act 3): confrontation, cost, ambiguous coda

---

## Writing guardrails for scene expansion
- Start scenes in routine behavior, not thesis dialogue.
- Keep conflict two-layered: practical surface + moral subtext.
- Maintain no-villain design.
- Use recurring motifs (red pen, rituals, thresholds/doors) with evolving meaning.
- End scenes with cost, not explanation.
