# Silent Cradle (Akuaba) — Lore Bible (v1)

> Working draft (local). Source of truth: Drive PDFs (Project Description.pdf + Akuaba.pdf). This document will be pasted/uploaded into Drive once browser control is stable.

## 0) One-line logline (current)
When a Calgary teacher’s desperate search for motherhood leads her to find hope in a Ugandan fertility doll (an akuaba), her growing obsession forces her community to confront the thin line between healing and delusion.

## 1) Project facts (from Project Description)
- **Title:** Silent Cradle
- **Genre:** Psychological Drama
- **Format:** Feature Film (90–100 minutes)
- **Language:** English
- **Setting:** Calgary, Alberta, Canada
- **Target audience:** Adult 18–54; art-house + mainstream crossover
- **Production company:** Badex Media Ltd.

## 2) Core promise / dramatic engine
A grounded, moral-complexity drama (Farhadi-adjacent) where each character’s “reasonable” actions create unintended consequences.

**Engine:** Lina’s grief → coping ritual → attachment → obsession → intervention → rupture → ambiguous path to healing.

## 3) Themes (primary)
- Grief and meaning-making after loss
- Faith/hope vs obsession/delusion
- Ethics of intervention (when help becomes harm)
- Marriage under extreme stress; two styles of grieving
- Social construction of motherhood; reproductive autonomy vs pressure
- Cultural appropriation vs spiritual authenticity (and what “respectful” means in practice)

## 4) Tone & style rules (Farhadi-inspired)
- Naturalistic performances; moral ambiguity; no villains
- Cause-and-effect scenes: small choices → large consequences
- Minimal score (lean on environmental sound)
- Handheld intimacy, natural light, longer takes for complex interactions
- Withhold “certainty”: let the audience wrestle with competing truths

## 5) Setting & cultural texture (Calgary)
- Diverse urban Canadian landscape: schools, clinics, cultural shops, community gatherings
- Calgary winter and domestic interiors can mirror emotional containment

## 6) Key characters (v1)
### Lina Morrison (Lead)
- Late 30s; elementary school teacher
- Compassionate, intelligent, caring—makes her unraveling feel tragic rather than sensational
- **Need:** motherhood / meaning / control in the face of bodily betrayal
- **Arc:** grief → ritualized hope → obsession → forced reckoning → imperfect healing

### Rob Morrison (Supporting lead)
- Late 30s; profession TBD
- Loves Lina, but emotionally constrained; his own grief creates blind spots
- **Dilemma:** protect Lina’s coping vs confront delusion and risk the marriage
- Explores masculine grief responses; “fixing” vs feeling

### Elena Vasquez (Supporting)
- Mid-30s; teacher colleague
- Care + boundary issues: intervenes out of love but can escalate harm
- Learns limits of friendship; necessity of professional help

### Dr. Chen (Supporting)
- 40s; grief counselor/therapist
- Represents professional frame; must engage a client who resists being “treated”

### Kwame Asante (Supporting)
- 50s; cultural artifacts shop owner
- Bridge between cultures; locus for questions about authenticity, exchange, respect

## 7) The akuaba (object bible)
- **Function:** transitional object / talisman / mirror for Lina’s need
- **Meaning:** culturally specific fertility symbol; becomes re-contextualized in Lina’s life
- **Risk:** must be handled respectfully; avoid exoticism or “magic solves it”
- **Visual motif:** shrines, care rituals, placement in frame (comfort → dominance)

## 8) Story constraints / guardrails
- Avoid “horror movie doll” tropes; keep it grounded in grief psychology
- Avoid didactic speeches about appropriation; dramatize conflicting perspectives
- The community’s actions should be understandable even when wrong

## 9) Questions to answer in screenplay (to guide revisions)
1) What *exactly* does Lina believe the akuaba is doing for her—comfort, protection, fertility, identity?
2) At what moment does Rob move from support to alarm (and why then)?
3) What does Elena want (control? saving Lina? preserving the school community?)
4) How does Kwame’s ethics land—does he warn her? enable? try to educate?
5) What is the intervention scene’s moral “trap” (everyone is right, everyone is wrong)?
6) What does “healing” look like if motherhood doesn’t happen?
