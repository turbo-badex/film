## 0) Project context (1 paragraph for the consultant)

*Silent Cradle* is a 90–100 min psychological drama set in Calgary about Lina Morrison (late 30s, elementary teacher) who, after a loss tied to her inability to have children, buys a small carved fertility figure (currently labeled "akuaba") from a cultural artifacts shop and gradually builds a private ritual around it. The film's ethical center is the line between healing and delusion, and between culturally legitimate belief and one person's individualized compulsion. We want to get the object, its meanings, and its care **right**, not decorative.

## A) Origin, identity, terminology

1. Project materials currently say "Ugandan fertility doll" and "akuaba" (lore bible logline). Are the geography, community/people, terminology, spelling, and pronunciation accurate and mutually consistent? What exactly must change?
2. Which community or tradition does the object belong to, and which variations should the film avoid collapsing together?
3. For the story to be credible, what should Lina's cultural identity be — insider, diaspora member, adjacent community, or outsider? (Current script: white Canadian teacher, "near 17th" shop.)
4. Are the character names and implied backgrounds — especially **Kwame Asante**, a Calgary shop owner in his 50s — consistent with the intended cultural setting?

## B) Meaning, acquisition, handling

5. What meanings does the object carry beyond "fertility symbol" (protection, memorial, transitional, spiritual)?
6. Who traditionally makes, gives, receives, carries, displays, or cares for it? Under what conditions is **buying one from a shop** plausible?
7. Is the shop scene credible as written (v3 scenes 4, pages 229–297): the back card, seller behavior, cash sale, plain-paper wrapping, the "treat it with respect, or leave it here" warning?
8. What physical form, materials, markings, condition, and provenance should production design use? What must it NOT look like?
9. Which handling/placement/cleaning/prayer/wrapping practices are accurate — and which would be invented, disrespectful, or sensationalized? (Lina repositions it, wipes dust from its face, whispers to it, and once holds it "like prayer, then panic.")

## C) Character and dramatic ethics

10. What should Kwame ask, explain, refuse, warn about, or follow up on so he has **ethical agency** rather than exposition delivery?
11. Would a more credible acquisition path be a gift, family/community mediation, education, or a prior relationship instead of an impulse purchase?
12. How can the film dramatize cultural exchange/appropriation without making the cultural character a moral lecturer?
13. Is there a missing community or family relationship that would make the object's meaning **lived** rather than decorative?
14. *(Lead addition)* We are considering a **Kwame second entry** in Act 2 where Lina returns to the shop as her behavior escalates. What would a credible, respectful version of that encounter look like — and is it advisable at all?

## D) Representation and harm risk

15. Does the current progression risk equating the cultural object/practice with obsession, delusion, infertility shame, or mental illness? Which specific images or lines create that risk?
16. How should the film distinguish culturally legitimate belief/practice from Lina's idiosyncratic coping?
17. Which "mystical/ritual/shrine/prayer/fertility" language in our materials should be revised or contextualized?
18. What must be **explicit** for accuracy, and what may remain **spiritually ambiguous** without misleading the audience?
19. What would respectful synopsis language, marketing, props, credits, and consultant acknowledgment require?
