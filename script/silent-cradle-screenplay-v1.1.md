## Scene 1 — Classroom competence

**INT. ELEMENTARY CLASSROOM — LATE AFTERNOON**

Coats zip. Chairs scrape. Lina writes on the board in neat block letters.

**LINA**
Last thing. If you quote, cite. If you claim, prove. No shortcuts.

A STUDENT (10) raises a hand.

**STUDENT**
Miss, does "I just felt like it" count as proof?

A ripple of laughter.

**LINA**
It counts as honesty.
(beat)
Not proof.

Bell. Kids flood out.

Lina sits. Reflection papers. Red pen uncapped.

Quick marks:
- "too broad"
- "unclear"
- "show me"

Elena appears at the door with a mug.

**ELENA**
You’re still here.

**LINA**
I work here.

**ELENA**
Friday, staff shower. I put you on setup. You want out, say it.

Lina keeps marking.

**LINA**
It’s fine.

**ELENA**
Okay.

Elena lingers, then leaves.

Lina reads one student line: *"Sometimes adults pretend not to be sad so kids don’t worry."*

She writes "vague." Stops. Crosses it out.

Closes the folder.

## Scene 2 — Domestic procedure

**LINA**
No.

**LINA**
Yes.

## Scene 1 — The shop (entry without mysticism)

**INT. CULTURAL ARTIFACT SHOP — LATE AFTERNOON**

Narrow aisles. Wood shelves. Brass bowls. Handwritten tags. Quiet instrumental radio in the back.

Lina stands near a shelf of carved figures. She isn’t browsing like a tourist; she’s reading labels.

KWAME (50s), measured, observant, appears beside her with no sales urgency.

**KWAME**
If you want the story, read the back card first.

Lina flips a card. A small carved figure: flat circular head, compact body.

**LINA**
Akuaba.

**KWAME**
Mm.

**LINA**
Fertility symbol?

**KWAME**
Among other things.
(beat)
People simplify it.

Lina keeps reading.

**LINA**
Do people buy it for... belief?

**KWAME**
People buy it for many reasons.
Comfort. Tradition. Memory.
Sometimes hope.

Lina almost puts it back. Doesn’t.

**LINA**
And you? What do you think it does?

Kwame shrugs lightly.

**KWAME**
I think objects don’t do much by themselves.
People do.

He gestures to the card.

**KWAME** (CONT'D)
If you take it, don’t make it a trick.
Treat it with respect, or leave it here.

Lina nods. Pays cash.

Kwame wraps it carefully in plain paper — not gift-style, not ceremonial.

**KWAME**
No shortcuts.

Lina registers the phrase.

**LINA**
I teach. I hate shortcuts.

She takes the wrapped object and leaves.

## Scene 1 — Dr. Chen offers a real exit

**INT. DR. CHEN’S OFFICE — LATE AFTERNOON**

Warm lamp. Tissue box untouched. A white-noise machine hums softly.

Lina sits upright, hands clasped, posture closer to a staff meeting than therapy.

DR. CHEN (40s) reads from intake notes, then closes the folder.

**DR. CHEN**
I’m not here to take hope away from you.

Lina gives a guarded nod.

**LINA**
Good.

**DR. CHEN**
I am here to ask whether your coping is reducing suffering... or reorganizing your life around fear.

A beat.

**LINA**
That sounds like a fancy way to call me irrational.

**DR. CHEN**
No.
(beat)
It’s a way to measure cost.

Lina looks away.

**DR. CHEN** (CONT'D)
Would you be willing to try one week of structured reset?
No object rituals after 9 PM.
One transparent check-in with Rob per night.
One day with no avoidance behaviors.

Lina almost laughs.

**LINA**
"No avoidance behaviors" isn’t medicine.

**DR. CHEN**
No. It’s data.
If your system helps, it survives contact with openness.
If it collapses, that tells us something too.

Lina takes this in. She’s moved, but defensive.

**DR. CHEN** (CONT'D)
You can keep dignity and still be honest.
Those are not enemies.

Long silence.

**LINA**
I’ll think about it.

**DR. CHEN**
Thinking is fine.
Just don’t call postponement "thinking."

Lina stands, nods once, exits.

That’s exactly how you talk to everyone now. Like they’re contractors.

We’ll keep this focused on conduct and student impact.

Yes.

## Scene 3 — Parking lot confession (too late cost)

**EXT. DISTRICT OFFICE PARKING LOT — LATE MORNING**

Cold sun. Traffic hiss from nearby road.

Lina exits the building alone. Rob is waiting by his car.

He studies her face.

**ROB**
How bad?

**LINA**
Not fired.
(beat)
Not clean.

Rob nods.

**ROB**
That sounds about right for us lately.

Lina almost smiles. Doesn’t.

**LINA**
I told them I confused control with professionalism.

**ROB**
Did you tell yourself that?

Long beat.

Lina finally looks at him fully.

**LINA**
I was trying not to be the woman people pity.

Rob says nothing.

**LINA** (CONT'D)
If I kept everything precise, maybe nobody could see...

She can’t finish.

**ROB**
See what?

Lina exhales — this is the line she has avoided all film.

**LINA**
That I’m ashamed.

Silence.

**ROB**
Of what happened to us?

**LINA**
Of what didn’t happen.

Rob closes his eyes briefly. Pain, not surprise.

**ROB**
I needed that months ago.

Lina nods, tears held but not released.

**LINA**
I know.

**ROB**
I can’t step back into the old house because this sounded right one day.

Lina accepts it.

**LINA**
I’m not asking for that.

**ROB**
What are you asking?

**LINA**
I’m asking you not to turn this into my final shape.

**ROB**
I won’t.

**ROB** (CONT'D)
Are you going to be okay today?

Lina answers honestly for the first time.

**LINA**
No.
(beat)
But I think I can be responsible.

Rob nods once and gets in. Drives away.

Lina stands alone in the lot, steadying herself.

Phone buzzes: message from Elena — *If you want soup, I can drop it at your door. No talking required.*

Lina reads it. Doesn’t reply yet.

She starts walking.

## Scene 3 — Ambiguous coda (object unresolved, cost clear)

**INT. APARTMENT BEDROOM — NIGHT**

Low lamp. Quiet. No score.

Lina opens the storage box on the bed.

Inside: clinic forms, old sonogram pamphlet, a photo strip of her and Rob laughing years ago.

She places the photo strip on dresser.

The akuaba sits nearby.

She picks up the object, wipes a thumb across its face as if cleaning dust—or comforting herself.

She sets it down.

Then moves it away from center, to the side of the dresser.

Not hidden. Not shrine-like.

She opens a notebook and writes one line:

> "Truth without cruelty."

She stares at the words.

Tears rise, but she stays composed.

Her phone buzzes.
Message from unknown parent number:
*"Maya came home proud today. Thank you for seeing her."*

Lina reads it twice.

She looks at the akuaba.

Long, unreadable gaze — gratitude? dependence? farewell? all three?

She turns off the lamp.

In darkness, city light from the window outlines both Lina and the object in the same frame.

Hold.

Cut to black.
