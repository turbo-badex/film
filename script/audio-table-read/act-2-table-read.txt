ACT II

SCENE 7: CLASSROOM DRIFT

INT. ELEMENTARY CLASSROOM — MORNING

Rain taps the windows. Students hand in short reflections.

Lina collects papers with her usual efficiency, but her tone is tighter than normal.

LINA
No names on top means no mark. We’ve covered this.

A STUDENT fumbles for a pencil.

STUDENT
I forgot, miss—

LINA
Then write faster.

The room goes still.

Lina hears herself. Softens half a degree.

LINA (CONT'D)
You have two minutes.

Later. During silent reading, Lina marks papers.

Red comments:
- "unclear"
- "where is your evidence?"
- "avoid emotional language"

She reaches one student page:

> "My mom says sometimes people act strict when they are scared."

Lina writes "speculative." Stops. Scratches it out.

Writes instead: "Give an example."

Elena appears at the door with an attendance sheet.

ELENA
Need your numbers for tomorrow’s assembly.

Lina signs without looking up.

ELENA
You okay?

LINA
I’m working.

ELENA
That wasn’t the question.

Lina slides the sheet back.

LINA
Then ask me after 4:30.

Elena nods, uneasy, and leaves.

CUT TO:

SCENE 8: HOME CROSSFIRE (CONTROL LANGUAGE)

INT. APARTMENT KITCHEN — NIGHT

Steam from a pot. Rob sets two bowls down.

Lina enters from bedroom, checks the akuaba placement on the shelf before sitting.

Rob notices.

ROB
You moved it again.

LINA
The light was wrong.

ROB
It’s a wooden figure.

LINA
And?

ROB
And lately it gets more attention than me.

Lina stirs soup she hasn’t tasted.

LINA
Don’t do that.

ROB
Do what?

LINA
Make this about jealousy.

ROB
It is about us.

A beat.

ROB (CONT'D)
You canceled Tuesday and didn’t tell me.

Lina doesn’t look up.

LINA
I rescheduled when it makes sense.

ROB
When it makes sense for who?

LINA
For reality.

ROB
Reality is we keep postponing the hard thing.

Lina pushes her bowl away.

She stands, takes her bowl to sink untouched.

ROB
You hear yourself?

LINA
Yes. I wish you did.

She exits to bedroom.

Rob stays seated, staring at two full bowls.

CUT TO:

SCENE 9: CONCERN BECOMES SOFT SURVEILLANCE

INT. SCHOOL CORRIDOR / ADMIN NOOK — LATE AFTERNOON

End-of-day noise. Lockers closing. Custodian cart rattling.

Lina is at the copier, scanning graded pages. Red marks dominate margins.

VICE PRINCIPAL SINGH (40s, controlled, not hostile) approaches with a neutral smile.

SINGH
Long day?

LINA
Like every day.

Singh gestures to the stack.

SINGH
Parents are asking for more "growth language" in feedback.

LINA
Meaning?

SINGH
Less corrective tone. More coaching tone.

Lina keeps feeding pages into scanner.

LINA
I coach them by correcting them.

Singh nods, careful.

SINGH
I know your standards are high. This is just... climate management.

Lina finally looks up.

LINA
So I should grade for climate.

SINGH
Grade for learning. Package for parents.

A beat. Singh lowers voice.

SINGH (CONT'D)
And if you need schedule flexibility, ask early. Don’t vanish calendar blocks.

Lina’s jaw sets.

LINA
I don’t vanish.

SINGH
Good. Let’s keep it that way.

Singh leaves.

Lina watches her go, then pulls one paper from the stack and reads her own note out loud under breath:

LINA
"Avoid emotional language."

She flips to another paper, writes in red:

"Be precise."

Then, in smaller letters beneath it:

"But don’t disappear."

She freezes, crosses out the second line hard.

Shoves the paper back into stack.

SCENE 10: DR. CHEN OFFERS A REAL EXIT

INT. DR. CHEN’S OFFICE — LATE AFTERNOON

Warm lamp. Tissue box untouched. A white-noise machine hums softly.

Lina sits upright, hands clasped, posture closer to a staff meeting than therapy.

DR. CHEN (40s) reads from intake notes, then closes the folder.

DR. CHEN
I’m not here to take hope away from you.

Lina gives a guarded nod.

LINA
Good.

DR. CHEN
I am here to ask whether your coping is reducing suffering... or reorganizing your life around fear.

A beat.

DR. CHEN
No.
(beat)
It’s a way to measure cost.

Lina looks away.

DR. CHEN (CONT'D)
Would you be willing to try one week of structured reset?
No object rituals after 9 PM.
One transparent check-in with Rob per night.
One day with no avoidance behaviors.

Lina almost laughs.

LINA
"No avoidance behaviors" isn’t medicine.

DR. CHEN
No. It’s data.
If your system helps, it survives contact with openness.
If it collapses, that tells us something too.

Lina takes this in. She’s moved, but defensive.

DR. CHEN (CONT'D)
You can keep dignity and still be honest.
Those are not enemies.

Long silence.

LINA
I’ll think about it.

DR. CHEN
Thinking is fine.
Just don’t call postponement "thinking."

Lina stands, nods once, exits.

CUT TO:

SCENE 11: HOME OPENING (THE REPAIR PATH APPEARS)

INT. APARTMENT LIVING ROOM — NIGHT

No TV. Rain at the window. Rob waits at the table with two mugs of tea.

Lina enters, sets keys down, sees he stayed up.

ROB
How did it go?

LINA
Fine.

He doesn’t move.

ROB
Fine like fine?
Or fine like "don’t ask me again"?

Lina sits. For once, no immediate deflection.

LINA
She asked me to do a one-week reset.

Rob leans forward, hopeful but careful.

ROB
Okay.

LINA
No rituals after nine.
Check-ins. Transparent schedule.

ROB
That sounds... possible.

Lina’s eyes fill, but she keeps her voice flat.

LINA
I don’t want to be managed.

ROB
I don’t want to manage you.
I want to stop losing you while you’re in the room.

Silence.

Lina takes a breath that could become honesty.

LINA
I am terrified all the time.

Rob nods, almost relieved to hear anything real.

ROB
I know.

LINA
No, you don’t.

The window closes. Lina retreats again.

LINA (CONT'D)
Tomorrow I have assembly duty at ten-thirty.
Don’t wait up.

Rob’s shoulders drop.

ROB
We were there for one second.

Lina stands.

LINA
I’m tired.

She exits to bedroom.

Rob sits with both mugs untouched.

CUT TO:

SCENE 12: MIDPOINT CHOICE: IMAGE OVER REPAIR

INT. SCHOOL ADMIN OFFICE — NEXT MORNING

Glass walls. Diplomas. Controlled quiet.

Vice Principal Singh and Elena sit across from Lina. Not a formal disciplinary meeting — but close.

A printed sheet on table: "Parent Feedback Themes — Communication Tone."

SINGH
We need alignment before this escalates.

Lina scans the sheet.

Quoted comments:
- "Too harsh."
- "My child feels judged."
- "Feedback lacks warmth."

ELENA
I told them your standards are strong.
This is about packaging.

Lina sets the sheet down.

LINA
So we’re grading my tone now.

SINGH
We’re protecting classroom trust.

LINA
By asking me to sound less honest.

ELENA
No. By asking you to sound reachable.

A long beat. This is the fork.

Singh slides a draft support option:
- temporary load adjustment
- optional coaching observation
- wellness leave resources

SINGH
Take this and we reset cleanly.
No notes on your file if we do it now.

Lina stares at the page. This is the open door.

Then she pushes it back.

LINA
I’m declining all of it.

ELENA
Lina—

LINA
I am fully capable of doing my job.
I don’t need a fragility plan.

SINGH
Then we document this meeting and proceed formally if concerns continue.

Lina nods, jaw set.

LINA
Do what you need.

She stands to leave, then turns back.

LINA (CONT'D)
For the record, precision is not cruelty.

She exits.

INT. SCHOOL WASHROOM — CONTINUOUS

Lina locks a stall, sits on closed lid, phone in hand.

Draft text to Rob:
I’m trying. I’m scared. I don’t know how to stop controlling everything.

She stares at it.

Deletes.

New text:
Running late. Don’t wait for me.

Send.

She presses palms to her eyes, breathes, then composes herself.

Unlocks stall. Neutral face restored.

SCENE 13: PARENT CONFERENCE (OPTICS TIGHTEN)

INT. SCHOOL MEETING ROOM — AFTERNOON

Small round table. Student work samples. Water cups untouched.

Lina sits with a PARENT COUPLE and Vice Principal Singh observing quietly from the side.

A printed assignment is on the table with Lina’s red comments.

PARENT (MOTHER)
We’re not saying standards are bad.
We’re saying this tone is... a lot.

PARENT (FATHER)
He’s ten. He read "imprecise" and "unsupported" like he failed as a person.

Lina keeps her voice measured.

LINA
The comments are about the work, not your son.

MOTHER
He cried before school.

A beat.

LINA
I’m sorry he felt that way.

FATHER
That sounds rehearsed.

Singh steps in softly.

SINGH
Let’s focus on repair. Ms. Morrison can provide revised feedback language and a confidence plan.

Lina turns to Singh.

Lina nods, but her jaw hardens.

LINA
I can revise the wording.

MOTHER
Thank you.

The mother relaxes slightly. Singh takes a note.

Lina sees it.

She smiles a thin professional smile.

LINA
Anything else for the record?

The room goes still.

CUT TO:

SCENE 14: ELENA OVERREACHES (CARE TURNS INVASIVE)

INT. STAFF PARKING LOT — EARLY EVENING

Cold wind. Cars starting. Elena catches up to Lina by her car.

ELENA
Hey. Don’t go yet.

Lina unlocks the car but doesn’t open it.

LINA
I’m tired, Elena.

ELENA
I know. That’s why I called someone.

Lina turns.

LINA
Called who?

ELENA
Dr. Chen’s office.
(quick)
Not details, just... concern.

Silence. Lina’s face empties.

LINA
You called my therapist.

ELENA
I called as your friend.

LINA
No. You called as my handler.

ELENA
That’s not fair.

LINA
Fair?
You made me a case file in a parking lot.

Elena tears up but holds ground.

ELENA
I watched you disappear in plain sight.
What was I supposed to do?

LINA
Ask me. Once. Then believe my answer.

ELENA
Your answer is always "I’m fine."

Lina opens the car door.

LINA
Exactly. And that should’ve told you where the line is.

She gets in, locks door, drives off.

Elena stands in exhaust and silence.

CUT TO:

SCENE 15: RUMOR CIRCULATION / HOME FALLOUT

INT. APARTMENT LIVING ROOM — NIGHT

Dim lamp. Rob sits with phone in hand, tense. Lina enters, drops bag.

ROB
Did something happen at school?

Lina freezes.

LINA
Why?

ROB
My sister texted me.
Her friend teaches in your district.

Lina says nothing.

ROB (CONT'D)
She heard you had a parent complaint and "wellness concerns." 

Lina laughs once — sharp.

LINA
Amazing.

ROB
Is it true?

LINA
What part? That parents complain? Or that women who don’t have children are apparently unstable now?

ROB
I didn’t say that.

LINA
You didn’t have to.

Rob stands.

ROB
I’m trying to stand next to you.
You keep treating me like press.

Lina goes to the shelf, straightens the akuaba by a few millimeters.

ROB (CONT'D)
There. That.
Every time we’re close to a real conversation, you fix an object.

Lina turns, voice low and precise.

LINA
Because objects don’t misquote me.

Rob absorbs that like a hit.

ROB
I don’t know how to reach you anymore.

Long silence.

LINA
Then stop trying to do it through everyone else.

ROB
I didn’t ask for this to become public.

LINA
Neither did I.

She walks past him to bedroom and closes the door softly — not slammed, worse.

Rob stands alone.

Phone buzzes on table.

On screen: SINGH — "Need to confirm tomorrow's meeting attendance."

He stares at it, doesn’t touch.

SCENE 16: FORMAL PROCESS LINE CROSSED

INT. SCHOOL CONFERENCE ROOM — MORNING

Rectangular table. Fluorescent light. A file folder sits in front of each person.

Present: Lina, Vice Principal Singh, HR REP (neutral), and Elena as witness/support.

The atmosphere is procedural, not hostile.

HR REP
This is a support meeting with documentation. Not disciplinary at this stage.

Lina nods once, expression flat.

SINGH
We need a temporary communication plan, parent-facing language calibration, and optional leave resources.

A paper slides toward Lina.

Header: Interim Support Agreement

Bullet points:
- supervised feedback review (2 weeks)
- no direct parent escalation without admin
- wellness consult check-in

Lina reads. Still.

HR REP
You can propose edits.

LINA
If I sign this, am I acknowledging impairment?

HR REP
You’re acknowledging support needs during a high-pressure period.

LINA
That’s just cleaner wording for the same thing.

Elena leans in.

ELENA
Lina, this can protect you.

Lina turns to her.

LINA
From what? Other people’s discomfort?

Singh keeps tone even.

SINGH
From escalation.

A long beat.

Lina closes the folder, pushes it back.

LINA
Then escalate.

Silence.

HR REP
To be clear, you’re declining interim supports.

LINA
Correct.

She stands.

SINGH
If this goes formal, there’s less room to move.

Lina gathers her bag.

LINA
I’m not going to perform fragility for this room.

She exits.

Elena shuts her eyes briefly.

CUT TO:

SCENE 17: COMPULSION EXPOSED IN CLASSROOM

INT. CLASSROOM — AFTERNOON

A student presentation is in progress. Poster board trembles in a child’s hands.

STUDENT PRESENTER
...and that’s why I think families can look different and still be—

Lina interrupts.

LINA
Evidence first. Feeling second.

The room chills.

A STUDENT in front row (Nora-equivalent age in earlier drafts; here: MAYA, 10) raises hand slowly.

MAYA
Miss, can I ask something?

LINA
About the presentation.

MAYA
You always say "be precise," but now it sounds like you’re mad before we even start.

A few kids look down.

Lina blinks, caught.

LINA
Continue, please.

The presenter continues, voice small.

STUDENT PRESENTER
Families can be... still good even if—

Lina’s phone buzzes on desk.

Display: ROB (3 missed calls)

She flips it face down.

Maya watches her do it.

Bell rings. Students leave quickly.

Lina sits. Red pen in hand. She writes on the presenter’s sheet:

"Structure weak. Argument diluted by sentiment."

She stares at the sentence.

Then writes beneath it:

"Revise with clarity."

Then, impulsively, adds:

"Do not confuse sympathy with truth."

She tears the page out. Realizes what she did. Breath catches.

She shoves it into drawer and slams it shut.

CUT TO:

SCENE 18: THE IRREVERSIBLE RUPTURE AT HOME (DARK TURN)

INT. APARTMENT LIVING ROOM — NIGHT

No lights except kitchen spill. Rob sits on couch, drained, folder on coffee table.

Lina enters late, sees the folder.

LINA
You went through my school documents?

ROB
Singh called me.

Lina goes cold.

LINA
Without my consent.

ROB
They said there may be formal action. They asked if I can support stability at home.

Lina laughs once, disbelieving.

LINA
So now my marriage is a compliance tool.

ROB
Don’t do this.

LINA
You talked to them about me.

ROB
Because you won’t talk to me.

He stands.

ROB (CONT'D)
I asked one thing for months: tell me what’s happening.
Instead you freeze me out and call it strength.

Lina points to shelf where akuaba sits.

LINA
At least that doesn’t betray me.

The words hang there. Too far.

Rob is stunned quiet.

ROB
You mean that?

Lina says nothing.

ROB (CONT'D)
Okay.

He grabs an overnight bag from under table — already packed.

LINA
What is this.

ROB
The part where I stop pretending this is temporary.

He moves toward door.

LINA
If you leave now, don’t come back to negotiate from the hallway.

Rob stops at door but doesn’t turn.

ROB
That’s exactly how you talk to everyone now. Like they’re contractors.

He exits.

Door closes.

Silence.

Lina stands still, then walks to the shelf and picks up the akuaba with both hands.

For a moment it looks like prayer.

Then it looks like panic.

Her phone lights up:
EMAIL — HR NOTICE: Formal review initiated. Attendance required Monday 8:30 AM.

She stares at it, breathing shallow.

Doesn’t open.

Holds the object tighter.
