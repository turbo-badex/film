# Silent Cradle — Synopsis Draft (Grant Version)

Lina, a respected elementary teacher in Alberta, has built a life on precision and control. In public, she is competent and admired. In private, she and her husband Rob are quietly cracking under the strain of infertility and the social expectations around motherhood.

When Lina forms an attachment to an akuaba doll as a coping ritual, she finds temporary relief. But what begins as private comfort starts bleeding into her relationships and judgment. At school, her rigid need for order hardens. At home, logistics replace intimacy. Small evasions become larger acts of narrative control as she tries to protect her image and avoid naming her grief.

As concern from friends and colleagues turns into scrutiny, Lina is repeatedly offered chances to repair. She declines, doubling down until the emotional cost becomes irreversible. In the final movement, she is forced to confront what she has truly defended: not healing, but ego and social survival.

*Silent Cradle* is a psychological drama about shame, control, and relational consequence. It avoids supernatural certainty and ends with moral clarity but emotional ambiguity: the audience can see movement, but not closure.

Length target: ~90 minutes.
