## 1) Logline (1-2 lines)
An Edmonton teacher coping with recurrent pregnancy loss turns to a culturally significant fertility object; what begins as hope spirals into obsession that threatens her marriage, friendships, and sense of self.

## 2) Short Synopsis (1 paragraph)
Set in Calgary/Edmonton context, *Silent Cradle* follows Lina as grief and social pressure around childlessness intensify after repeated miscarriages. Anchored in emotional realism with restrained folkloric elements, the story explores how private coping rituals can gradually destabilize relationships. As Lina’s fixation deepens, her marriage with Rob and trust with Elena fracture under accumulated silence, shame, and control. The film interrogates belief, identity, and the cost of unresolved grief.

## 3) Full Story Synopsis (1-2 pages)
- Setup: Lina and Rob navigate fertility loss while presenting normalcy in public/professional life.
- Inciting incident: Lina adopts a private ritual linked to an akuaba fertility symbol as a form of agency and hope.
- Escalation: Ritual reliance grows; communication deteriorates; Rob and Elena respond differently, increasing tension.
- Crisis: Lina’s behavior and secrecy trigger relational rupture and emotional isolation.
- Climax: Lina confronts the consequences of obsession versus vulnerability and relational repair.
- Resolution / ending tone: Ambiguous but emotionally grounded ending where the personal cost is clear even if belief remains unresolved.

## 4) Three-Act Structure
### Act I
- Establish marital strain, social pressure, and internalized grief.
- Introduce ritual object and first signs of fixation.

### Act II
- Escalating conflicts with Rob and Elena; boundaries erode.
- Attempts at control intensify; denial and defensiveness deepen.

### Act III
- Emotional confrontation and irreversible relational consequences.
- Ambiguous final note around belief, with clear human cost.

## 5) Character Arcs
- Lina: From grief-stricken but functional to increasingly controlled by ritualized coping; final confrontation with cost of isolation.
- Rob: From supportive restraint to boundary-setting and emotional fatigue; seeks realism and relational honesty.
- Elena: Functions as relational mirror/challenge; pressures truth-telling and accountability.

## 6) Core Themes and Dramatic Conflicts
- Theme 1: Grief, shame, and reproductive identity
- Theme 2: Belief versus control
- Central conflict: Intimate relationships strained by unprocessed loss
- Internal conflict: Lina’s need for agency versus acceptance of uncertainty
- Relational conflict: Love/care versus secrecy/obsession

## 7) Tone and Style Direction
- Tonal references: A Separation-style realism with restrained symbolic texture.
- Visual style notes: Naturalistic environments, intimate framing, psychologically charged quiet moments.
- Narrative style notes: Slow-burn emotional escalation, grounded dialogue, minimal sensationalism.
- Audience experience intent: Empathy, tension, reflection on care, belief, and human limits.

## 8) Why This Story Now
The film engages urgent conversations around fertility grief, mental health, and cultural identity in immigrant and broader Canadian contexts, with practical relevance to families and communities often underrepresented in mainstream narratives.
